Ember Ledger

Fire records change when satellites change. That makes it hard to tell whether an area had more burning activity or simply more detections.

Ember Ledger is a calendar that helps land managers compare those records, examine unusual periods and inspect the evidence.

THE PROBLEM, IN ONE DAY

Different counts.
A shared grid.

999MODIS detections
3,755VIIRS detections

In the Dixie Fire study area on August 5, 2021, these observations occupy 98 shared-grid cells at 4 km. Both sensors saw 76 of them.

Each cell counts once per day. Sensor agreement stays visible, and the seasonal reference is 15 cells across 105 comparison days.

THE LONG RECORD

25 years.
Two kinds of evidence.

Explore 1,086,752 retained NASA observations from 2001–2025 in and around Northern California.

The year-by-month calendar shows observed activity. A separate adjusted view estimates 2001–2011 totals for the full study region, with clear labels and empirical error ranges.

Older estimates are modeled totals. Map cells and source downloads remain satellite observations.

At 4 km, a seasonal MODIS adjustment reduces mean absolute error against the combined record from 12.95 to 7.15 cells per day.

This tests agreement within the archive. Earlier-year transfer remains an assumption, and smaller-area models were less consistent.

DATA, LIMITS & NEXT STEPS

Make every claim checkable.

Clouds, overpass timing, outages and processing changes affect detections. Empty cells can contain fire. This is historical analysis, not a forecast or a burned-area estimate.

Methods, source hashes, the Python pipeline and validation results are public. Next steps are observation-coverage masks and independent testing before applying the adjustment to other areas.
