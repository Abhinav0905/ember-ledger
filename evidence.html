PROJECT EVIDENCE

A fire record you can inspect.

Ember Ledger helps land managers examine historical burning activity without confusing every satellite detection with a separate fire. This page connects the working prototype to the five Space Apps criteria and states what remains unproven.

Try one exampleFive criteriaScope and limitsSix-slide demo

A one-minute path through the evidence

1. Open the Dixie example. In the atlas, choose Dixie Fire. August 5, 2021 is preselected. At 4 km, 999 MODIS and 3,755 VIIRS detections occupy 98 cells, with 76 seen by both. These are different measures: detections are observations; cells group them spatially.

2. Check the conclusion at three scales. The selected-day table compares 2, 4 and 8 km grids. This example exceeds its seasonal reference at all three sizes. That makes the flag less dependent on the chosen grid, but does not prove that every fire was detected.

3. Keep the evidence. Download the review brief for the selected area and day. It includes counts, all three grid checks, bounds, reference definition, source links and limits. Date-range exports retain the original NASA observation fields.

4. Inspect the harder claim. Choose Adjusted history for the full region, then open validation. Older estimates are labeled. Their error ranges and transfer assumptions remain visible.

Evidence against the five criteria

The criteria come from the official Judging & Awards Guide. The entries below describe evidence and gaps, not self-awarded scores.

What the reported improvement means

At 4 km, daily mean absolute error against the combined observed record falls from 12.95 to 7.15 cells, a 44.8% reduction across 13 held-out years. Each held-out year improves. This is evidence for a specific count adjustment within this archive. It is not 44.8% better wildfire detection or a measure of lives, area or property saved.

The simpler unadjusted input is the baseline. See every yearly result, the formulas and remaining bias.

Implemented scope and next tests

Working now: a regional historical atlas, experimental aggregate backcast and reproducible downloads. Proposed use: retrospective seasonal review. Not included: live alerts, forecasting, operational dispatch or incident attribution.
  1. Test the workflow with land managers. Record whether they can explain the sensor change and recover the source evidence without help.
  2. Add coverage masks and compare against an independent reference appropriate to the question. Burned area and active-fire detections are different quantities.
  3. Add a clearly dated current-year feed, with archive and near-real-time products distinguished.
  4. Validate transfer to another region before exposing adjusted values for custom areas.
